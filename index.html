About Me

Much like Neo in The Matrix, I don't just see the world as it appears—I see the code behind it. I am a PhD Scholar at the Robotics Research Center, IIIT Hyderabad, advised by Dr. K. Madhava Krishna. My life's work is viewing reality through the lens of algorithms, attempting to model the messy, high-entropy distribution of the real world.

The "Grand Prix" of Chaos: My current research targets decision-making for Autonomous Vehicles. But I’m not building for the predictable racing lines of Formula 1; I’m building for the Indian roadway. Driving here is less about following rules and more like facing a constant cricket googly. To survive, an agent must read the bowler’s hand—or in my case, the pedestrian’s latent intent—before the action even happens.

My Approach: I am developing a hybrid planning architecture that fuses safety guarantees with social intelligence. Using Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL), my models infer the hidden "reward functions" of other drivers. This gives the robot a computational "Spidey Sense"—allowing it to predict likely behaviors and negotiate space in a chaotic environment.

Origin Story: My journey began at the University of Georgia (Master's in AI). Think of this as my "Muggle Studies" arc. I dove into Philosophy and Psychology alongside Computer Science because I realized that to navigate a human world, an AI needs to understand the "human" operating system. My master's thesis was my version of Defense Against the Dark Arts: I built an Explainable AI (XAI) framework to cast a "Lumos" spell on black-box Deep RL policies, making their decisions transparent.

The Vision: We have enough algorithms that are book-smart; I want to build ones that are street-smart. My goal is to decode the unwritten rules of human interaction, creating systems that don't just calculate trajectories, but actually understand the culture they drive in.
